# carelolo.com — Search Console audit

Audited September 13, 2026, using the signed-in Chrome session, Google Search Console, Google URL Inspection, PageSpeed Insights, and selected live pages.

**Assessment: search visibility is developing, but organic search traffic remains small and branded searches are its clearest strength. There is no manual penalty or host outage reported. The highest-value work is to connect and improve the local service pages, resolve the legacy-content URL inventory, and improve mobile delivery. Customer acquisition and bookings require separate conversion data.**

The review covered all 194 displayed Web query rows, all 107 Web page rows, the complete 40-day daily table, country/device/search-appearance breakdowns, Web/Image/Video/News totals, the generative-AI report, every indexing exclusion category, submitted-page indexing, sitemaps, robots, crawl stats, links, HTTPS, Core Web Vitals, breadcrumbs, video indexing, all three removal tabs, manual actions, security issues, and relevant settings. Live content review sampled the homepage, companion-care article, companion/respite/overnight service pages, and cost page. This is a comprehensive Search Console review with selected live-page checks, not a live HTTP crawl of every site URL or an analytics/conversion audit.

## Highest-impact content and navigation findings

**1. Connect the local service pages to the main customer journey.**

The live homepage links to profiles, registration, resources, about, and FAQs, but no direct link to the Raleigh companion/respite/overnight service pages was present in its rendered navigation or footer. The reviewed companion-care article links to other informational guides and `/get-care`, but not `/raleigh-companion-care`.

The service page is already indexed. URL Inspection confirms successful crawling, self-declared and Google-selected canonical agreement, sitemap discovery, and one valid breadcrumb. Its listed referring pages are other local service/trust pages. It has only four performance impressions and one click. This is a distribution and usefulness opportunity rather than a confirmed indexing block.

Add a clearly labeled local-services entry on the homepage and contextual links from the companion, respite, overnight, and cost articles to the matching service pages. Keep helpful editorial cross-links as well. Do not treat the fragment URLs shown in performance, such as `/#how`, as proof of duplicate-page indexing.

**2. Bring the service-page experience up to the standard of the newer site.**

The sampled companion, respite, overnight, and cost pages use “HomeCare” branding and a visible **“All Raleigh SEO pages”** section. Their substantive content consists largely of brief generic blocks followed by two overlapping groups of service links. The newer homepage and companion article use LoLo branding and explain the offer more clearly.

Replace the internal SEO label with a useful customer-facing navigation label, consolidate repetitive link groups, and align branding and booking language. Give each service page distinctive information: actual service area, what is included/excluded, visit length, realistic availability, current price conditions, caregiver-selection details, and a concrete next step. Use genuine operational information and supported proof; do not invent testimonials or availability.

**3. Make the cost page answer the cost question.**

`/home-care-cost-raleigh-nc` discusses cost drivers without a dollar figure or worked example, including after expanding all three FAQs. The homepage currently advertises “Starting at $30 per hour” and one-hour visits; the companion article repeats that with conditions. Bring verified current pricing and illustrative visit budgets onto the cost page, explaining applicable limits and fees. Its current performance is only nine impressions, zero clicks, position 35.3.

**4. Improve snippets where there is enough evidence.**

The homepage title is “LoLo Care | Trusted help at home”; its description says “Find and coordinate flexible, non-medical home support for someone you love.” Consider a clearer locality/service signal while preserving the brand. A candidate to review is “LoLo Care | Companion & Home Support in Raleigh-Durham.”

For pages with relevant impressions near the first page, examine their specific queries before editing titles: Durham transportation (59 impressions, position 8.3), caregiver burnout (80, 10.9), and non-medical caregiver duties (77, 14.4). Avoid chasing unrelated `lolo.com` queries or treating a handful of low-impression averages as stable rankings.

Live evidence: [companion service](https://carelolo.com/raleigh-companion-care), [companion article](https://carelolo.com/blog/what-is-companion-care-triangle-families), [cost page](https://carelolo.com/home-care-cost-raleigh-nc), [homepage](https://carelolo.com/).

A separate warning identifies **`/caregiver/register` as indexed despite a robots.txt block**. The fetched robots editor explicitly shows `/caregiver/`, `/login`, `/register`, and other application paths disallowed. Blocking crawling does not reliably remove an already known URL from search. If exclusion is intended, implement a crawlable `noindex` strategy for public authentication pages; preserve authentication controls for private content. Do not broadly open private application routes. [Google's noindex guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/block-indexing).

The “crawled, currently not indexed” validation failed August 22 after starting August 14. This is an issue-validation outcome, not a manual penalty. The URLs span resource types and do not all need to become indexed HTML pages.

The submitted-pages view shows **68 indexed and 9 not indexed** in that report snapshot. The nine comprise four discovered URLs and five legal pages excluded by noindex. Remove intentionally excluded legal URLs from the current sitemap if they remain there; otherwise allow the report to refresh. The five are:

That newer count should not be subtracted from the older 68-indexed count to claim 18 indexing failures. Direct XML opening was blocked by the browser client, so the current XML contents were not independently verified. Google reports all four host/protocol robots files fetched successfully without issues; the main HTTPS file was checked September 12.

Crawl stats show **1,728 requests** (1,710 apex host and 18 www), approximately 49.1 MB downloaded, and **110 ms average response time**. Both hosts report no problems. Responses are 81% HTTP 200, 12% 404, 5% 302, 2% 301, and smaller other categories. The 110 ms measure is crawl-response timing, not a browser page-load score. Discovery is 10% and refresh 90%; this small site does not demonstrate a severe crawl-capacity shortage.
