# LoLo Care — Brand DNA v3

**Version 3.0 • Production-ready • August 2026**  
**Primary users:** Leadership, product, growth, PR, content, social, partnerships, support, and approved external agencies  
**Document owner:** LoLo Care  
**Review rule:** Revalidate pricing, geography, trust, legal, feature, and metric claims before every campaign launch.

## Document purpose

This is LoLo Care’s single messaging source of truth. It defines the durable brand core and the exact boundaries for public claims. It is designed to help anyone writing or speaking for LoLo Care sound consistent, useful, and credible without making the company appear larger, safer, more automated, or more widely available than current evidence supports.

The strategic principle behind v3 is simple: **trust is built by precision**. Warmth attracts attention; accuracy earns permission to keep it.

## How to use this Brand DNA

- Start with the approved brand core, positioning, and message pillars.
- Choose the audience and format, then adapt from the approved messaging hierarchy.
- Check every factual statement against the claim decision matrix.
- Use APPROVED wording as written or with a meaning-preserving edit.
- Use QUALIFIED claims only with the stated condition or limitation.
- Do not publish HOLD claims until the named evidence and owner approval exist.
- Treat changing facts—prices, fees, markets, caregiver counts, visits, customer numbers, and product capabilities—as governed claims, not evergreen brand language.

This document does not replace legal review, finance approval, product release notes, or operational launch approval. When any of those sources conflict with this document, the current approved source wins and this Brand DNA must be updated.

## The non-negotiable idea

LoLo Care serves older adults and families choosing among managing alone, using a marketplace, or engaging a home care agency. Home care agencies are direct competitors for many of the same non-medical help-at-home needs. LoLo Care’s distinction is a technology-enabled marketplace and coordination model that combines caregiver choice, flexible access that can often begin within 24 hours, a transparent $30/hour family total, and shared visibility.

The brand must make older adults and families feel that they can take the next practical step without pretending that a marketplace removes every risk or solves every care need.

# 2. Category and company definition

## Approved category definition

LoLo Care is a **technology-enabled marketplace and coordination platform for flexible, non-medical help at home**.

This definition is intentionally specific:

- **Marketplace** explains that older adults and families connect with caregivers through the platform.
- **Coordination platform** expresses the shared schedules, communication, payment, and visit-information value.
- **Flexible** communicates that support can be arranged around changing needs rather than only through a long-term care package.
- **Non-medical** sets a critical scope boundary.
- **Help at home** is plain, human language that covers the practical use case without sounding institutional.

This business-model definition does not place agencies outside the competitive set. LoLo Care competes directly with home care agencies for older adults and families choosing how to arrange non-medical help at home.

## What LoLo Care is

- A place for older adults and families to discover independent caregivers offering non-medical support.
- A workflow for reviewing profiles, requesting or booking help, communicating, coordinating, and paying.
- A shared family view that can reduce fragmented texts, calendars, and follow-up.
- A source of profile-level trust signals and visit information that can support better decisions.
- A product designed around practical support for aging at home.

## What LoLo Care is not

- A home care agency.
- A staffing agency, nurse registry, or employer of caregivers.
- A healthcare provider or clinical service.
- An emergency response service.
- A guarantee of caregiver performance, safety, availability, or continuity.
- Continuous location surveillance.
- A substitute for medical advice, emergency services, or an individualized care plan from a qualified professional.

## Internal category shorthand

Use **“trust-and-coordination platform”** internally and in strategic conversations. In public copy, pair it with the plain-language explanation “for flexible, non-medical help at home” so the audience does not have to decode a category phrase.

# 3. Audience system

## Core ICP 1: older adults

Older adults are a core customer group, including people who recognize their own need, research options, choose a caregiver, book and pay for support, or invite family members to help coordinate. They are decision-makers and users of the service—not merely the subject of someone else’s care plan.

### What they are trying to accomplish

- Get practical help while preserving independence, choice, and control.
- Find support that fits their routine, preferences, home, and budget.
- Review caregiver information before deciding whom to contact or book.
- Arrange companionship, errands, rides, meals, light household help, respite, or routine assistance.
- Decide whether and how trusted family members participate in coordination.

### What they need to hear

- “The choice remains yours.”
- “Start with the practical support that fits your life.”
- “Review caregiver information before you decide.”
- “You can involve family members without giving up control.”
- “The $30/hour family total, approved split, and next action are clear.”

## Core ICP 2: the family organizer

Family organizers include adult children, relatives, or trusted people who notice a need, start the search, compare options, coordinate schedules, keep others informed, or help an older adult manage the arrangement.

They may be balancing work, children, distance, finances, and multiple family opinions. Their core tension is not simply “find a caregiver.” It is: **find appropriate help, make a confident decision, and keep the arrangement working without becoming the entire operating system.**

### What they are trying to accomplish

- Find practical support that matches a loved one’s needs and schedule.
- Understand who is available and what information is known about them.
- Avoid a complicated, high-commitment process for a manageable need.
- Coordinate with siblings or other trusted people.
- Know whether a visit is scheduled, underway, or complete.
- Keep communication and payment from scattering across multiple tools.

### What they need to hear

- “You can take one practical step.”
- “You can review relevant information before choosing.”
- “You do not have to coordinate every detail alone.”
- “The scope, pricing, and next action will be clear.”
- “This is non-medical help, and the limits are explicit.”

## Secondary audience: family collaborators

These are siblings, spouses, relatives, neighbors, or other trusted people who share responsibility or need visibility. They may not choose the caregiver, but they need a reliable view of schedules, messages, and visit information.

Their primary need is **shared context without another group chat becoming the system of record**.

## Supply audience: independent caregivers

Caregivers want a credible way to present their experience, availability, services, and relevant trust information; connect with older adults and families; manage requests; communicate; and receive payment through a clear process.

The brand should respect caregivers as skilled adults and service providers. It must not frame them as interchangeable labor, promise earnings, or imply an employment relationship that the legal model does not support.

## Press and community audiences

Journalists, partners, and community organizations need a concise explanation of the problem, business model, product, scope, and evidence. They should receive the same truth as customers—never a more inflated version.

# 4. Customer problem and jobs to be done

## The human problem

Practical needs often arrive one at a time: a ride, a meal, companionship, errands, light household help, or someone to be present for a few hours. The family response, however, can become a second job. One person searches, screens, schedules, pays, checks in, relays updates, and answers everyone else’s questions.

The burden is both logistical and emotional. Older adults want useful support without losing agency; family organizers want to respect that independence while feeling confident that needed support is in place.

## Functional jobs

- Help me identify caregivers who may fit the need.
- Help me compare relevant profile information.
- Help me request or arrange support without unnecessary commitment.
- Help me keep schedules, communication, payment, and visit information together.
- Help my family share visibility without duplicating coordination.

## Emotional jobs

- Help me feel that I am responding responsibly.
- Help me move from worry to a concrete next step.
- Help me preserve my loved one’s dignity and choice.
- Help me share responsibility rather than quietly absorbing it.

## Social jobs

- Help me explain the plan clearly to siblings or other trusted people.
- Help me show that decisions are based on visible information, not guesswork.
- Help our family act like a coordinated team.

# 5. Positioning system

## Positioning statement

For older adults seeking practical support at home and family organizers helping arrange it, LoLo Care is a marketplace and coordination platform that helps them find independent caregivers, arrange flexible non-medical help, and coordinate the details—while preserving the older adult’s choice and reducing the burden on any one person.

## Brand idea

**Trusted help at home, without becoming the care manager.**

Use this as the primary strategic message and a strong public-facing line. It works because it joins the desired outcome—trusted help—with the hidden pain—the coordination burden.

## Brand promise

**More confidence, less coordination burden.**

This is a promise about the intended product experience, not a guarantee of care outcomes. Copy should support it with concrete mechanics rather than repeating it as a slogan.

## Strategic position

**The trust-and-coordination layer for aging at home.**

This is the clearest strategic distinction. Search alone is not enough. Scheduling alone is not enough. A family needs a way to evaluate options and coordinate what happens next.

## Differentiation

LoLo Care’s differentiation is the combination of:

1. Flexible access to practical, non-medical help.
2. Visible caregiver information and profile-level trust signals.
3. A shared coordination experience for the family.
4. Clear scope, pricing presentation, and visit workflow.

Home care agencies are direct competitors because older adults and families may consider both models for the same practical support needs. Many traditional agency models can involve longer intake or scheduling lead times and less flexible arrangements. LoLo Care should differentiate through its marketplace model, caregiver choice, transparent $30/hour split, family coordination experience, and service that can be arranged within 24 hours in most cases, subject to caregiver availability.

Do not reduce differentiation to a single unsupported superlative. The brand wins by making the combined experience useful and credible.

# 6. Message pillars

## Pillar 1: Practical help that fits real life

### Meaning

Older adults and families do not always need a long-term package or clinical care. They may need a few hours of companionship, errands, rides, meal preparation, light household help, respite, or routine assistance.

### Supporting product truth

- Non-medical service categories are visible in the product.
- Older adults and families can browse caregiver availability and see the platform-set hourly total.
- The family pays $30 per hour in total: $27 goes to the caregiver and $3 is LoLo Care’s platform fee. In most cases, service can be arranged within 24 hours, subject to caregiver availability, location, requested service, and requested time.

### Approved language

- “Flexible, non-medical help at home.”
- “In most cases, service can be arranged within 24 hours, subject to caregiver availability.”
- “Companionship, errands, rides, meal preparation, light household help, and everyday support.”
- “Book from one hour, subject to caregiver availability and the current booking flow.”

### Avoid

- “Instant care.”
- “A caregiver whenever you need one.”
- “No waitlist,” “24-hour guaranteed,” or “same-day guaranteed.”
- Any language that implies medical or emergency care.

## Pillar 2: Choose with clearer information

### Meaning

Trust should be expressed as useful information and transparent signals, not as a blanket guarantee. Older adults and families can review profiles and available indicators before deciding whom to contact or book.

### Supporting product truth

- Profiles can show experience, services, availability, ratings, pricing, identity-verification status, and background-check status.
- The product has distinct identity and background-check indicators.
- Secure payment and structured communication can reduce informal transaction risk.

### Approved language

- “Review caregiver profiles, availability, ratings, pricing, and available verification information.”
- “Profiles can display identity-verification and background-check status.”
- “Choose with more context.”
- “Relevant trust signals, visible before you decide.”

### Avoid

- “Every caregiver is fully vetted.”
- “Guaranteed safe.”
- “LoLo-approved caregivers” unless a documented approval standard supports the phrase.
- Using “verified” without specifying what was verified.

## Pillar 3: Keep the family coordinated

### Meaning

The product is designed to reduce fragmented coordination. Family members can share relevant schedules, messages, and visit information instead of depending on one person to relay every update.

### Supporting product truth

- The public product describes a shared family view.
- Family members can be invited into the coordination experience.
- Scheduling, messaging, payment, visit status, and caregiver-submitted information can live in one workflow.

### Approved language

- “Keep schedules, messages, payments, and visit updates in one place.”
- “Invite trusted family members to share visibility.”
- “Know when support is scheduled and when a visit is complete.”
- “Caregiver-submitted visit information helps the family stay informed.”

### Avoid

- “Automatic visit transcription.”
- “Continuous GPS monitoring.”
- “Know exactly what is happening at every moment.”
- “Replace the care manager” or any claim that the product assumes professional care-management duties.

## Pillar 4: A clear path from need to next step

### Meaning

Older adults and families should understand what LoLo Care offers, what it does not offer, how pricing is presented, and what happens next. Clarity is a conversion tool and a trust signal.

### Supporting product truth

- Caregiver profiles show hourly pricing.
- The platform-set family total is $30 per hour: $27 to the caregiver and a $3 LoLo Care platform fee.
- The booking and payment workflow presents the $30 total and its split before confirmation.
- LoLo Care’s non-medical scope can be explained plainly.

### Approved language

- “The family pays $30 per hour in total: $27 goes to the caregiver and $3 is LoLo Care’s platform fee.”
- “See the $30 total and its split before you confirm.”
- “Find care” and “Become a caregiver” as clear role-based actions.
- “Non-medical help for everyday needs.”

### Avoid

- Any statement that adds another platform fee on top of the $30 family total.
- Any split other than the approved `$30 family / $27 caregiver / $3 LoLo` standard without current finance approval.
- Hidden or euphemistic descriptions of fees.
- Agency, employment, medical, or insurance language not supported by the operating model.

# 7. Product truth and proof system

## Approved product truths

Subject to current availability and the live product experience, LoLo Care may say that older adults and families can:

- browse caregiver profiles;
- review services, experience, availability, ratings, hourly pricing, and available verification information;
- request or arrange non-medical support;
- communicate through the platform;
- pay through the platform’s payment flow;
- coordinate schedules and visit information;
- invite trusted family members into a shared view;
- receive arrival, completion, and caregiver-submitted visit updates supported by the current workflow.

## Qualified product truths

- **Verification:** Name the indicator. Say “identity-verification status” or “background-check status,” not simply “verified.”
- **Location:** Say “location-confirmed check-in or check-out when device permission is available.” Do not imply continuous tracking.
- **Visit summaries:** Say “caregiver-submitted visit notes,” “visit information,” or “visit updates.” Do not imply ambient or automatic capture.
- **Availability:** Say “subject to caregiver availability.” Do not promise an immediate match.
- **Shared family view:** Describe visibility and coordination. Do not imply professional case management or medical recordkeeping.
- **Independent caregivers:** Say “independent caregivers” and make clear that LoLo Care does not employ them.

## Proof hierarchy for public claims

1. Implemented product behavior verified in the current release.
2. Current finance, operations, trust, and legal records.
3. Dated metric exports with definitions and accountable owners.
4. Customer or caregiver stories with written permission and fact verification.
5. Current authoritative external research with direct citations.

An agency presentation, brainstorming note, old strategy document, or unsourced spreadsheet is not proof.

# 8. Voice and tone

## Brand personality

### Warm

Recognize the human weight of arranging help. Sound caring without becoming sentimental or presumptuous.

### Steady

Reduce anxiety through clear next steps. Avoid urgency tricks, fear, and exaggerated reassurance.

### Specific

Name what the product does. “Compare profiles and availability” is stronger than “a seamless care solution.”

### Respectful

Preserve the agency of older adults, families, and caregivers. Do not describe aging as helplessness or caregivers as interchangeable supply.

### Credible

Use the exact level of confidence the evidence earns. Qualification is not weakness; it is professional discipline.

## Writing principles

- Lead with the real-life task, not the technology stack.
- Prefer one clear claim over three inflated adjectives.
- Use short sentences when explaining scope, pricing, or trust.
- Pair emotional language with a concrete product mechanism.
- Explain what the user can do, see, or decide.
- Keep the older adult present as a person, not merely the object of family logistics.
- Use “may,” “can,” and “subject to” only when a meaningful condition exists.
- Never hide a product limitation behind vague language.

## Before and after examples

**Avoid:** “LoLo revolutionizes senior care with fully vetted caregivers and real-time GPS tracking.”  
**Use:** “LoLo Care helps older adults and families find non-medical help, review caregiver information, and stay informed through arrival and visit updates.”

**Avoid:** “Get instant care for only $30.”  
**Use:** “Families pay $30 per hour total: $27 to the caregiver and a $3 platform fee. In most cases, service can be arranged within 24 hours, subject to caregiver availability.”

**Avoid:** “Our automatic visit summaries make sure nothing is missed.”  
**Use:** “Caregiver-submitted visit information helps trusted family members stay up to date.”

**Avoid:** “The easiest way to manage your elderly parent.”  
**Use:** “A clearer way to arrange practical support with an aging loved one.”

**Avoid:** “LoLo’s caregivers are safer than agency caregivers.”  
**Use:** “Profiles can show relevant experience, ratings, pricing, and available verification information so older adults and families can decide with more context.”

# 12. Trust-and-safety messaging guardrails

## The rule

Describe observable trust signals and platform processes. Do not promise safety as an outcome.

## Approved formulations

- “Profiles can display identity-verification and background-check status.”
- “Review experience, services, availability, ratings, pricing, and available verification information.”
- “Secure payment through the platform.”
- “Structured visit and communication information can help families stay informed.”

## Required qualifications

- State which verification or check is being referenced.
- Confirm the indicator is visible and current for the specific profile or population being described.
- Avoid implying that a background check is exhaustive, perpetual, or a guarantee.
- Route universal screening statements, safety comparisons, and legal interpretations through the trust owner and counsel.

## Prohibited shortcuts

- “Fully vetted.”
- “Guaranteed safe.”
- “The safest caregivers.”
- “Every caregiver is background checked” without current population-level proof.
- “LoLo takes responsibility for caregiver conduct” unless counsel provides the exact statement.

# 13. Pricing messaging guardrails

## Approved customer-facing wording

**The family pays $30 per hour in total: $27 goes to the caregiver and $3 is LoLo Care’s platform fee. The $30 total and its split are shown before confirmation.**

## Rules

- Never describe $30 as a starting rate or imply that caregivers set different hourly rates.
- Never add another platform fee on top of the approved $30 family total.
- State the approved standard split accurately: $27 to the caregiver and $3 to LoLo Care as the platform fee. Verify any non-standard case—such as an approved promotion, refund, tax treatment, or pricing change—with finance before publishing it.
- Never compare LoLo’s price with an agency, competitor, or national average without a dated like-for-like methodology.
- Recheck the live configuration and checkout calculation before every pricing campaign.
- If the platform-set rate changes, update every public pricing reference and the claim matrix before publication.

## HOLD pricing claims

- Any split that differs from `$30 family / $27 caregiver / $3 LoLo` without current finance approval.
- “LoLo is 33% cheaper than an agency.”
- “Agencies keep two-thirds.”
- “Caregivers earn nearly twice as much.”
- Any savings or earnings claim without a documented comparison method.

# 14. Geography and availability guardrails

## Approved baseline

LoLo Care is currently available in Wake County, North Carolina, and plans to expand nationwide. Actual service availability depends on caregiver availability, location, requested service, and requested time.

## Rules

- A caregiver profile visible in a city or state is not sufficient proof that LoLo has formally launched that market.
- Do not group Wilmington or Lincolnton into the Research Triangle.
- Use “LoLo Care plans to expand nationwide” as a forward-looking statement, not as a claim of current national availability.
- Market-launch language requires an owner, launch date, service boundary, support readiness, and current supply confirmation.
- Do not publish a nationwide launch date, national coverage claim, or additional live-market list until operations approves it.

## Availability language

Approved wording: **“In most cases, service can be arranged within 24 hours, subject to caregiver availability.”** Availability also depends on location, requested service, and requested time. Avoid “instant,” “on demand,” “always available,” “no waitlist,” and any guaranteed 24-hour or same-day promise.

# 15. Competitor and comparison guardrails

## Strategic approach

Home care agencies are direct competitors: older adults and families may choose between an agency and LoLo Care for similar non-medical help-at-home needs. Many traditional agency models can involve longer intake or scheduling lead times and less flexible arrangements. LoLo Care can contrast its flexible marketplace model and qualified 24-hour service advantage without attacking agency quality or making a guarantee.

## Safe contrast dimensions

- Flexible hourly support and service that can be arranged within 24 hours in most cases, subject to caregiver availability, versus longer or less flexible intake and scheduling models.
- A $30/hour family total, split as $27 to the caregiver and a $3 platform fee, versus an agency quote only when the agency price, fee basis, geography, service scope, and date are documented.
- Direct caregiver choice through a marketplace versus an agency-led matching and service model, described factually.
- Shared family coordination versus fragmented texts, calendars, and payment tools.
- Profile-level information and trust signals versus choosing with less visible context.
- Non-medical practical help versus clinical or emergency care, as a scope distinction rather than a value judgment.

## Requirements for a named comparison

- Use current public sources from both parties.
- Compare the same geography, service type, duration, fee basis, and date.
- Separate facts from interpretation.
- Give the competitor a fair description.
- Obtain legal and leadership approval before publication.

## Prohibited comparison behavior

- Invented agency economics.
- Unsupported quality or safety claims.
- “Only,” “best,” “cheapest,” or “first” without defensible substantiation.
- Presenting a national benchmark as a local quote.

# 18. Spokesperson FAQ

## What is LoLo Care?

LoLo Care is a marketplace and coordination platform for flexible, non-medical help at home. It helps older adults and families find independent caregivers, review relevant profile information, arrange support, communicate, pay, and share schedules and visit updates.

## Is LoLo Care a home care agency?

No. LoLo Care is a marketplace and technology platform, not a home care agency, staffing agency, healthcare provider, or emergency service. It does compete directly with home care agencies for many of the same non-medical help-at-home needs.

## Does LoLo Care employ caregivers?

No.

## Are all caregivers background checked?

Do not make a universal statement unless current records support it. The accurate baseline is that profiles can display identity-verification and background-check status, allowing older adults and families to review the information available for a specific caregiver.

## How much does care cost?

The family pays $30 per hour in total: $27 goes to the caregiver and $3 is LoLo Care’s platform fee.

## How much do caregivers earn?

Under the approved standard split, the caregiver receives $27 per hour and LoLo Care receives a $3 platform fee from the family’s $30 hourly total.

## Where is LoLo Care available?

LoLo Care is currently available in Wake County, North Carolina, and plans to expand nationwide. Do not imply that nationwide availability exists today or promise a launch date without operations approval.

## Does LoLo Care provide medical care?

No. LoLo Care is designed for non-medical help such as companionship, errands, rides, meal preparation, light housekeeping, respite, and routine assistance. It is not a healthcare provider or emergency service.

## Does LoLo Care track caregivers in real time?

Do not describe continuous tracking. The current workflow can support location-confirmed check-in or check-out when device permission is available, with a manual fallback.

## Are visit summaries generated automatically?

The approved current description is caregiver-submitted visit information, notes, and status updates. Do not claim ambient listening, automatic transcription, or AI-generated summaries unless that capability is implemented and approved for public description.

## What makes LoLo Care different?

LoLo Care combines flexible access to practical, non-medical help with profile-level information and a shared family coordination workflow. The value is not only finding help; it is keeping the next steps, communication, payment, and visit visibility easier to manage.

## Can LoLo Care guarantee a caregiver is available?

No. In most cases, service can be arranged within 24 hours, but timing depends on caregiver availability, location, requested service, and requested time. Do not guarantee a match or a specific start time.

## How big is LoLo Care today?

Use only a current, dated, owner-approved metric export with a clear definition. If that evidence is not available, describe the product and market problem without quoting a size or growth number.

# 19. Claims approval workflow

## Required claim record

Before a new factual claim becomes public, record:

1. Claim ID and exact proposed wording.
2. Claim state: APPROVED, QUALIFIED, or HOLD.
3. Definition and calculation method, if numeric.
4. Primary source and source location.
5. Data as-of date or feature version.
6. Accountable owner.
7. Legal, finance, trust, product, or operations approval as applicable.
8. Permitted channels and required qualification.
9. Expiration or next review date.

## Approval path

1. The writer proposes the exact claim.
2. The accountable functional owner validates the source.
3. Legal reviews regulated, comparative, safety, worker-classification, privacy, testimonial, and guarantee-adjacent language.
4. Finance reviews pricing, savings, earnings, revenue, GMV, and payout language.
5. Operations reviews market, availability, screening, support, and supply claims.
6. Product reviews feature behavior and release status.
7. The claim owner records the final approved wording and review date.
8. The publishing team uses the approved form without broadening its meaning.

## Recommended review cadence

- Dynamic counts and growth metrics: every 30 days and before each use.
- Pricing, fees, features, and geography: every 90 days and after any change.
- Trust, privacy, legal, and caregiver-relationship language: every 90 days and after any policy or process change.
- Testimonials and customer stories: before each reuse if circumstances or permissions may have changed.
- Evergreen positioning and voice: annually or after a material strategy change.

## Expiration rule

An expired claim returns to HOLD until re-verified. Previous publication does not preserve approval.
