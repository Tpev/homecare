# Wake County Offline GTM Strategic Memo

Date: April 4, 2026
Execution week: Monday, April 6, 2026 through Friday, April 10, 2026
Owner: Sales / community partnerships

## Purpose

This memo defines the offline go-to-market plan for Wake County for the week of April 6, 2026.

The goal is not general awareness. The goal is to create trusted local distribution channels that put HomeCare in front of families already caring for an aging parent.

## Business Context

HomeCare already has meaningful caregiver-side supply in Wake County. That changes the GTM problem.

The question is no longer "Can we recruit caregivers?"

The question is now:

"How do we get Wake County families to hear about HomeCare from places they already trust, understand what HomeCare is, and take a first step quickly?"

## Strategic Thesis

Offline growth in Wake County should be built around trust transfer, not mass flyer volume.

The highest-value channels are:

1. Senior centers and active adult centers
2. Community centers with strong adult programming
3. Churches with senior adult or pastoral care ministries
4. Caregiver-serving nonprofits and referral organizations
5. Community networks that can introduce HomeCare to multiple institutions at once

Families in this category often move when one of the following happens:

- hospital discharge
- rehab return home
- daytime supervision gap
- adult child living at a distance
- caregiver burnout
- need for companionship, check-ins, meal prep, errands, or non-medical support

Our outreach should therefore be framed as:

"HomeCare is a local Wake County resource for families who suddenly need non-medical in-home support for an aging parent."

## Primary Goal For This Week

Build a repeatable offline channel system, not a one-time street team effort.

This week must produce:

- approved flyer placements
- newsletter and bulletin inclusions
- speaking or tabling opportunities
- warm introductions to people who guide families
- a small set of trusted institutions willing to treat HomeCare as a legitimate local resource

## Success Metrics For April 6-10, 2026

Minimum acceptable output:

- 25 physical stops
- 15 real conversations with staff, directors, pastors, or ministry leaders
- 10 approved flyer placements
- 5 booked follow-up meetings
- 4 booked talks, resource tables, or ministry introductions
- 3 newsletter or bulletin inclusions
- 2 referral-list or partnership conversations

High-confidence output:

- 2 to 4 institutions willing to regularly expose families to HomeCare
- 1 to 2 institutions willing to host a short educational session
- 1 nonprofit or network organization willing to discuss a broader partnership path

## What We Are Selling

We are not selling "a marketplace."

We are selling a clearer local outcome:

- trusted non-medical support at home
- Wake County caregiver availability
- help for adult children coordinating care
- companionship, check-ins, meal prep, errands, and home support
- a next step for families who need help quickly

## Positioning Statement

Use this wording consistently:

"HomeCare helps Wake County families quickly find non-medical in-home support for an aging parent. We are especially useful when a family needs help after discharge, needs daytime check-ins, or when adult children are coordinating care from work or from out of town."

## Rules For The Salesperson

1. Do not lead with "Can I leave flyers?"

Lead with:

"Who handles family resources, community materials, or caregiver support information here?"

2. Do not make the interaction feel transactional.

The tone must be:

"We want to be useful to your families."

3. Do not pitch clinical care.

Always say "non-medical home care support."

4. Do not overclaim.

Never imply:

- agency staffing
- Medicare coverage
- clinical home health
- nursing services
- immediate guaranteed placement

5. Always ask for the next higher-value step.

Ask in this order:

- flyer placement
- newsletter or bulletin inclusion
- table at an event
- short talk or educational session
- introduction to the right staff member

6. Every stop must end with a next step.

Examples:

- "I will email the flyer by 3 p.m."
- "I will follow up Tuesday morning."
- "We will send over a short blurb for the bulletin."
- "Can we tentatively hold a 10-minute slot for April 16?"

## Channel Priorities

### Priority 1: Senior and active-adult centers

Why:

- high concentration of older adults
- established trust
- clear programming calendar
- bulletin boards, newsletters, and events
- many already work with caregiver-facing partners

### Priority 2: Churches with senior ministries or pastoral care

Why:

- family caregiving problems surface quietly in faith communities
- churches can distribute through pastors, office staff, bulletins, and ministry leaders
- trust is relational, not purely informational

### Priority 3: Caregiver-serving nonprofits

Why:

- high credibility
- strong referral influence
- can unlock multiple families and institutions

### Priority 4: Community centers

Why:

- lighter-trust channel than senior centers, but still useful
- good for bulletin board presence, family events, and civic legitimacy
