# Round two: LoLo brand, contrast and interaction review

Reviewed September 7, 2026. **Design review and illustrative concept revision only; no application implementation.** This pass responds to the request for stronger contrast, closer LoLo branding and reliable mouse hover, then reassesses the proposed experience.

Read this alongside the [original UX audit and proposal](ux-audit-and-redesign-proposal.md), [data and action preservation map](data-preservation-map.md), [recurring-care audit](recurring-audit.md) and [detailed V1 interaction audit](round-two-interaction-audit.md). The original [175-state tracker](../master-tracker.md) and all application screenshots remain the unchanged baseline. They have not been replaced with concept images or marked as redesigned.

**Conclusion:** keep the simplified workspace and its task-first hierarchy, but make its visual language accurately LoLo and make every interaction state explicit. V2 demonstrates the revised direction with real local preview interactions. It is not a complete production UI, an implemented payment flow or proof that every baseline field is already represented.

## Brand evidence and decisions

The current [LoLo logo README](../../../../brand/lolo/README.md) specifies Warm Evergreen `#23483F`, Soft Ivory `#FFF7EA`, Clay Coral `#C96B55` and Deep Ink `#24302D`. It names the primary evergreen wordmark and the reversed ivory wordmark, and explains that their shapes are outlined SVG paths. The [application CSS](../../../../resources/css/app.css) uses the same evergreen/ivory/coral identity, with warm white cards and muted green-gray text. The [app layout](../../../../resources/views/layouts/app.blade.php) loads Inter and Source Serif 4; CSS applies them to body and headings respectively. The captured app navigation and pages visually support this reading.

The [Home Care HUB guide](../../../../docs/branding/home-care-hub-brand-guidelines.md) is a legacy-named reference. Its warm, calm tone, readable serif headings, rounded surfaces and restrained accents remain useful. Its teal/lavender palette differs from the current LoLo assets, so it should not override the shipped LoLo colors. Similarly, [tailwind.config.js](../../../../tailwind.config.js) lists Manrope/Outfit, but that conflicts with the explicit fonts loaded and applied in the actual app. V2 follows the current CSS and logo assets.

| Brand element | Decision for V2 | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Wordmark | Use the actual outlined [LoLo SVG wordmark](../../../../public/images/marketing/lolo/lolo-wordmark-evergreen.svg), with its original proportions; ivory treatment on the dark surface. | A generic serif rendering of “LoLo” is not the logo. Reusing the supplied paths retains its identity. |
| Primary action and headings | Evergreen `#23483F`; ivory `#FFF7EA` action text. | These are the current brand anchors and provide a strong readable pair. |
| Page and card surfaces | Ivory `#FFF7EA`, warm white `#FFFDFA` and restrained pale warm sections. | Preserve the warm care-service character without making the page visually flat. |
| Body and secondary text | Deep Ink `#24302D`; stronger muted text such as V2's `#485E53` on light surfaces. | Reading hierarchy should come from size, spacing and weight, without fading meaningful text. |
| Functional boundaries | Separate decorative divider and interactive-control tokens; V2 uses a darker light-mode control border `#748176`. | A faint card divider can remain subtle; an input or secondary action needs a more perceptible boundary. |
| Accent and status | Clay Coral stays a limited accent. Attention and destructive states use darker readable text on restrained tinted surfaces and explicit labels. | Small coral text on ivory is weaker than the evergreen pair. Color should support the state, not carry its meaning alone. |
| Fonts and shapes | Actual Inter and Source Serif 4; soft cards and rounded controls; comfortable control heights. | Match the product already captured while improving its information hierarchy. |

V2's dark appearance is an illustrative adaptation of these brand anchors, not a claim that a separate official dark-mode specification exists. Its final rendered contrast and controls need the same verification as the light appearance.

## Findings from V1 and the V2 response

| Priority | V1 finding | V2 change and remaining limit |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | **Primary button text nearly disappears on hover.** A generic button hover background wins over the primary variant while retaining the primary foreground. Static color calculations put the light primary near 9.4:1 normally but near 1.1:1 on hover; the dark example also approaches 1.1:1. | Give primary, secondary, text, selected and disabled controls explicit foreground/background/border states. Gate hover to enabled controls and appropriate pointer devices. Preserve the selected treatment while hovering. Rendered measurements are recorded separately below. |
| P2 | **Inputs and secondary controls have faint boundaries.** V1 uses the same light border for meaningful interactive boundaries and decorative separators; its light border against paper is approximately 1.33:1. | Use a stronger functional border token while retaining quiet decorative lines. Keep visible field labels and a distinct focus state. A stronger border does not require dark, heavy cards. |
| P2 | **The identity is approximate.** The wordmark is generic serif text; headings do not explicitly use the exact brand serif, and the first concept does not load the actual font families. | Reuse the official wordmark paths and load Inter/Source Serif 4. Follow the current LoLo palette rather than approximate greens or the older guide's lavender. Verify font loading in the rendered concept, not only the CSS declarations. |
| P1 | **Text drafts disappear when the view rerenders.** V1 replaces rendered content without retaining review/message values. A family could lose work while comparing tabs or switching layout. | Save drafts against the example care record and action; message/invitation drafts also include their caregiver context. Restore drafts on return. Preserve rating and disclosure state, and restore focus after navigation/filtering and closing an action. This is local in-memory demonstration, not persistent storage across reloads. |
| P1 | **Payment appears as a repeated commitment button without a complete decision surface.** Repeating the same charge CTA adds emphasis without giving the family a clear review step. | Use one hours-summary action that opens a local review with scheduled/recorded time, break, care amount, processing fee, total and consequence. The review has Back and one final confirmation label. Any resulting feedback explicitly remains a preview; no charge occurs. |
| P1 | **Help assumes the wrong stage.** V1 always discusses reviewing hours before payment, including before hire or during live care. It also lacks representative live completion/safety flows. | Render Help from the selected request/visit/plan stage: finding care, change/cancel before care, concern/support and deliberate end-visit during care, hours question after submission, billing/receipt and care concern after payment. Plan Help points to the affected dated visit or general support. These representative forms still need the full production fields and eligibility rules. |
| P2 | **Hover, selection, focus and disabled states are not consistently distinguishable.** Some navigation has no perceptible hover response, disabled styling relies on opacity, and rerendering can displace keyboard focus. | Retain native focus indication and add an offset ring. Show explicit enabled hover/pressed, selected and disabled colors. Avoid text/background opacity fades or whole-button brightness effects. Keep focus feedback independent of mouse hover and restore it to the relevant control after an action. |
| P1 | **The recurring parent and dated visit need an interactive distinction.** V1's plain breadcrumb does not open a parent example, and a single lifecycle can imply that ending the plan closes its pending visits. | V2 provides a parent-plan destination and exact dated-visit examples. Pending, active, paused and ended plan examples retain distinct meanings; an ended plan can retain its next confirmed visit, and outstanding hours/payment/review work remains visible. This illustrates the model; it does not cover every real status combination. |

The V1 contrast figures above are **static calculations from the defined token pairs**, not a complete rendered accessibility audit. They are approximate descriptions of the failure mechanism; filtering, inherited styles and actual theme resolution must be checked in the browser. The [interaction audit](round-two-interaction-audit.md) records the exact V1 pairs and cascade mechanism.

## How V2 should feel

The first view answers “What is happening, and what do I need to do?” A returning family sees the date, caregiver, state and any outstanding decision before the long care brief. One-time requests and recurring occurrences use the same stable local destinations: **Overview, Caregivers, Care details, Help**. A recurring plan uses **Overview, Visits, Care details, Help**. The selected destination remains clear on hover; the interaction should never temporarily erase the text to signal a click target.

The complete recipient profile remains in Care details, with the safety reminder and contact route close to current care. The current care version remains distinct from a later editable profile. The current action opens a concrete review or local form, with context and a working Back path. A payment review is separate from hours submission and from leaving a caregiver review. A plan change names its scope and keeps the dated visits visible.

The representative Help screens now ask about the task that exists at that stage. They do not promise that a pending invitation is booked care or that a cancelled/refunded/corrected visit was simply completed and paid. The remaining production exceptions are a required next design pass, not details to discard for a shorter screen.

## Rendered verification record

**Rendered verification completed.** The [browser verification record](round-two/validation.json) includes 451 checks of representative visit/plan views in light/dark at 320, 360, 390, 736 and 1024 CSS pixels, plus 168 one-time-care overflow checks at 320, 390 and 1024 pixels. The final record has no failed checks or JavaScript errors. These checks validate the illustrative concept, not the application or every production state.

| Check | Result/evidence |
|---|---|
| Primary normal/hover/pressed foreground and background | 9.54:1 normal, 11.84:1 hover, 13.71:1 pressed in light and dark. V1's resolved foreground/background pair was about 1.1:1 on hover; its whole-element brightness effect did not remedy that failure. [Light hover](round-two/v2-light-primary-hover.png), [dark hover](round-two/v2-dark-primary-hover.png). |
| Secondary, text, selected and disabled controls | Representative primary/secondary/text actions, current/other navigation, selected/unselected filters, rating and disclosure controls checked. Enabled text stays above 4.5:1 in the checked normal/hover/pressed states. Disabled hover leaves its colors unchanged; selected controls retain their distinct selection. |
| Muted text, functional border and focus ring | Minimum across 12,344 rendered text checks: 6.58:1. Input boundary: 4.02:1 light / 5.44:1 dark against its surface. Added focus ring: 6.65:1 light / 7.97:1 dark. Decorative separators stay softer. Native focus indication is retained. [Keyboard focus](round-two/v2-light-keyboard-focus.png). |
| Official wordmark and actual font loading | Official SVG wordmark proportions preserved; the browser confirms loaded Inter and Source Serif 4 faces. [Desktop](round-two/v2-light-1024.png), [mobile](round-two/v2-light-390.png). Dark presentation uses the same wordmark silhouette in ivory; it is a proposed theme adaptation. |
| Keyboard focus and Back restoration | Keyboard focus is visible; Back restores the payment entry or the correct candidate (including James rather than defaulting to Sofia). Candidate filters retain controls and reset with one/zero results. This is not a full screen-reader test. |
| Draft retention and record separation | Message draft survives tab navigation; review text/rating survive tabs and placement changes. Wednesday and Friday message drafts are separate. One-time and recurring review drafts/ratings are separate, and the original returns intact. Issue inputs use the same state storage; each complete production form still needs its own parity check. |
| Representative payment, hiring and Help previews | A payment entry opens a readable review with hours, fees, total and final action. Feedback explicitly says nothing was submitted. Candidate review and Back, stage-appropriate Help and general-support availability checked. [Mobile payment review](round-two/v2-touch-payment-390.png). Plan-management and several support panels remain explanatory previews rather than complete editors. |
| Plan/visit independence | Pending/active/paused/ended views checked. An ended plan retains Friday's confirmed visit; opening it and its change panel keeps Friday's date. Pending-plan Help offers support without requiring a nonexistent visit; paused care offers Resume. |
| Responsive layout | No horizontal overflow in the checked sizes. Actual touch context verified at 390px with mobile viewport metadata: hover rules inactive, visible button targets at least 44px, payment review opens by tap. [Touch/mobile view](round-two/v2-touch-390.png). Software-keyboard occlusion, 200% text and real-device assistive technology remain future checks. |

An illustrative flow can pass these checks while still lacking production states, complete data parity and assistive-technology validation. Record that distinction when reporting the results.

The audit uses 4.5:1 as the normal-text threshold, including hover/focus text, and 3:1 for necessary component/state indicators against adjacent colors. These are scoped checks based on the [W3C text contrast guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html) and [non-text contrast guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html). Passing these color checks does not establish WCAG conformance for the whole concept or app.

### Additional corrections caught during this pass

- Dated action panels now use the selected visit's date rather than a hard-coded Wednesday.
- Drafts and ratings are keyed by care variation and record context; moving to another visit does not reuse the first visit's draft.
- Back from another candidate's hire/decline panel restores that candidate's control.
- Paused-plan copy and upcoming rows no longer contradict each other; pending-plan Help does not require a booked visit.
- General support and care-concern routes remain reachable across booked stages alongside the immediate stage-specific task.
- The mobile harness was corrected to include viewport metadata; the final touch screenshot and measurements are from an actual 390 CSS-pixel layout.

## Five prioritized proposals before implementation

| Order | Priority | Next design work | Completion evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | P1 | **Build the real booking, correction and payment exception matrix.** Design scheduled-but-unprotected, missing check-in, paused/no-show/cancelled/disputed, submitted-unconfirmed, approval-saved/payment-needed, correction comparison/version/changes-requested, manual extra reports and partial/full refunds. Keep expected/recorded/corrected time, plan state, payment state and review state independent. | Each supported exception has a factual header, relevant action, original values/history, charge consequence and desktop/mobile screen. A pending correction cannot display its proposed hours as already confirmed. |
| 2 | P1 | **Complete plan-management forms.** V2 schedule/pause/resume/end panels are explanatory previews, not complete editors. Add selected weekday rows, start/end/duration, effective date, optional end/return dates, notes, affected-date comparison, acceptance state, skip-one and keep-next/cancel-next choices. | A reviewer can enter a proposal, inspect every affected and unaffected visit, return without losing values, and understand the exact scope before simulated confirmation. Preserve existing business rules from the recurring audit. |
| 3 | P1 | **Demonstrate all candidate facts and care details.** Compact cards need complete metadata behind their details: full reply, availability, location/proximity, rate/fees, experience, review count, reliability, skills/languages, match score/reasons, and verified versus reported credentials. Retain all profile/contact/task/location fields and invitation history states. | At least one dense, fully populated synthetic candidate and care brief, plus zero/one/many/filtered results, are reviewable without hidden omissions. “Details available” placeholder copy is not evidence of preservation. |
| 4 | P2 | **Test accessibility and comprehension with people.** Verify keyboard order, state announcements, rating single-selection semantics, focus restoration, zoom/larger text, touch targets, motion preferences and screen-reader behavior. Ask representative family users to identify the next action and explain its consequence without coaching. | Record observed confusion and successful completion across desktop/mobile, including older users and caregivers/family users with relevant accessibility needs. A contrast calculation alone is not usability evidence. |
| 5 | P1 release gate | **Prove field/action parity across the full 175-state baseline before implementing the production redesign.** Map each screen and tab to its new destination and check the preservation matrix; then cover the explicit source-only and external branches. Preserve permissions, profile snapshots, record IDs, drafts, statuses and existing histories. | A signed-off parity checklist with every field/action retained or an explicit reviewed decision; new separate after-change screenshots only when implementation actually exists. Keep the current 175-state application baseline unchanged as the reference. |

The recommendation remains to move forward with this direction after the second-pass checks, then fill these missing representative states before application work. The goal is a calmer, more readable LoLo workspace with complete, truthful care records and clear consequences at every decision.
